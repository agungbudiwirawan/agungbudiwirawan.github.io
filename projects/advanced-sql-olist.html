1 · Revenue growth

WITH monthly_revenue AS (
  SELECT FORMAT(o.order_purchase_timestamp, 'yyyy-MM') AS month,
         SUM(oi.price) AS revenue
  FROM orders o JOIN order_items oi ON o.order_id = oi.order_id
  WHERE o.order_status = 'delivered'
  GROUP BY FORMAT(o.order_purchase_timestamp, 'yyyy-MM'))
SELECT month, revenue,
  LAG(revenue) OVER (ORDER BY month) AS prev_month_revenue
FROM monthly_revenue;

Revenue in Jan–Aug 2018 is +141% vs the same months of 2017. November 2017 peaks at R$988K (+52% MoM) on Black Friday, then drops 26% in December.

2 · Seller ranking, and why I rebuilt it

The first version ranked sellers by revenue * 0.6 + avg_review * 0.4. When I checked it against its parts, the ranking was identical to a revenue-only ranking (Spearman ρ = 1.00). Revenue is in thousands of R$ while reviews are 1–5, so the review weight never mattered.

WITH scored AS (
  SELECT *,
      0.6 * PERCENT_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY revenue)
    + 0.4 * PERCENT_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY avg_review) AS score
  FROM seller_metrics
  WHERE total_orders >= 30)
SELECT *, RANK() OVER (ORDER BY score DESC) AS seller_rank
FROM scored;
Result: only 1 of the previous top 10 stays in the top 10 (628 sellers with 30+ orders), and the top 10's average review rises from 4.01 to 4.45. The v1 list included a top-revenue seller rated 3.35★.
